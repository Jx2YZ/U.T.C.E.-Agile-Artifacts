# :exclamation: Project Assumptions & Constraints 

This document sets out our **assumptions** and **constraints** for the Urban Tree Cover Explorer (**UTCE**).

## Assumptions (Scope)

### **Project Scope Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – The project scope will remain stable throughout the process, with little to no changes from the client.
- **Justification** – While we cannot prevent major changes, this assumption ensures we proceed with the project consistently, and in accordance with existing specifications.

### **Tool Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – Every team member will have access to tools such as WordPress, GitHub and collaboration platforms (Google Docs, Zoom, WhatsApp).
- **Justification** – A seamless workflow and development process will ensure we can efficiently build the product, track our work, and stay in touch to promptly resolve blockers.

### **Technical Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – We will be implementing the required features using WordPress and plugins provided by the client, rather than sourcing our own tools.
- **Justification** – Our scope is narrowed, and complexity is reduced, ensuring the team can proceed swiftly before relevant deadlines.

### **Stakeholder Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – The client will provide feedback and clarification when needed.
- **Justification** – We will require alignment on project goals and outcomes to ensure the product meets expectations and is delivered in compliance with deadlines.

### **Dependency Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – Any third-party tools we use (including any Wordpress plugins or themes, and any map datasets) will be actively maintained and supported by their own development teams, and vetted by our client before we use them.
- **Justification** – This is critical to avoiding security or compatibility issues, and therefore essential for ensuring project stability and avoiding major technical issues.

### **Quality Assurance Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – We will conduct regular testing to ensure the product complies with relevant specifications, including functional, usability, and performance testing to ensure a stable and efficient product.
- **Justification** – Testing helps us catch any bugs early-on, and ensures we deliver a high-quality product.

## Assumptions (Budget)

### **Resource Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – The team is available to put in the necessary hours per sprint to achieve our goals, and to actively participate in all meetings (including stand-ups, sprint reviews and retrospectives.)
- **Justification** – Consistent team collaboration will be required to ensure we stay on progress.

### **Cost Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – The client will not require the team to obtain additional resources at the team's cost.
- **Justification** – Consistent engagement with the client will be required to ensure we understand and cost-related constraints, and can effectively manage these. This ensures we can proceed with development promptly, and comply with relevant timeframes.

## Assumptions (Schedule)

### **Submission Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – Our submission deadlines will not change.
- **Justification** – This consistent deadline will mean we can plan adequately to ensure we can submit the final product, documentation, and demonstration video by the project deadline. This ensures compliance with project requirements and timely submission/product delivery.

### **Availability Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – The client will provide all required WordPress capabilities and plugins prior to the sprints in which they are required, noting we are reliant on their selected tools.
- **Justification** – The team needs to commence work promptly. Timely availability of key tools means we are able to work in accordance with our planned schedule, and meet key deadlines.

### **Deprioritisation Assumptions**
- **Assumption** – The client will promptly confirm whether any features are to be deprioritised, following our consultation with them regarding any relevant limitations.
- **Justification** – Prompt updates to feature requirements will enable the team to adjust relevant plans to ensure we can submit the final product, documentation, and demonstration video by the project deadline. This ensures compliance with project requirements and timely submission/product delivery.


---

## Constraints

### Time Constraints
- **Constraint** – we must deliver a minimum viable product (**MVP**) within 6 weeks (i.e. 3x2 week sprints), leaving us limited time to confirm our client's preferred approach, to develop materials, and practically develop the MVP.
- **Justification** - we must ensure core functionality (e.g. tree cover mapping) is available for early testing and (where possible) stakeholder feedback to ensure our MVP is acceptable.

### Resource Constraints
- **Constraint** – we are a development team of only 5 people, each with limited bandwidth to devote to the project (e.g. work, other study commitments etc.).
- **Justification** – we must ensure we work efficiently and collaboratively to balance capacity and drive the project forward within the above time constraints.

### Technical Constraints
- **Constraint** – we have varying levels of development experience, with limited knowledge of WordPress (being the selected hosting platform). 
- **Justification** – we must upskill quickly in order to deliver the project in compliance with the above time constraints.

### Budget constraints
- **Constraint** – we are not receiving funding to produce this project.  Rather, we are reliant on software licences and other materials provided by our client (the teaching team), and the accessibility of those materials.
- **Justification** – we must engage early and often with our client to ensure we have sufficient clarity over the materials available to us, and over constraints affecting our ability to engage with further tools or providers.

### System constraints (tree dataset)
- **Constraints** – we are reliant on an existing database of tree cover, with little flexibility to modify this to be more appropriate/adapted to our client's end goal.
- **Justification** – we must communicate these constraints to our client promptly to foreshadow any limitations in functionality (noting the scope of the systems the client requires us to use), and proposing alternatives where possible

### System constraints (parks dataset)
- **Constraints** – we are reliant on an existing database of parks and places of interest, with little flexibility to modify this to be more appropriate/adapted to our client's end goal.
- **Justification** – as above, , we must communicate these constraints to our client promptly to foreshadow any limitations in functionality (noting the scope of the systems the client requires us to use), and proposing alternatives where possible.

### System constraints (Wordpress)
- **Constraints** – we are reliant on the client's existing Wordpress environment, and are unable to modify global settings.  This may limit our flexibility to implement elements of the client's selected plugins (e.g. WP GO Maps), or other global settings (e.g. login management).
- **Justification** – as above, we must communicate these constraints to our client promptly to foreshadow any limitations in functionality (noting the scope of the systems the client requires us to use), and proposing alternatives where possible.

### Stakeholder constraints
- **Constraint** – our client is driving multiple projects at once, limiting their availability to provide commentary on the adequacy of our MVP.
- **Justification** – we must engage with our client early and often to address any blockers, and ensure our MVP meets their specifications.
